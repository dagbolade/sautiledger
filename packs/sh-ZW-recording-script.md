# SautiLedger — Shona (sh-ZW) recording script

*For our Shona validator — thank you! This takes about 30–40 minutes total,
in two short steps. You are the expert here: every "draft" line below is
only a starting point, and we expect you to change most of them.*

SautiLedger is a voice bookkeeping app for market traders. You talk your
sales ("I sold two buckets of maize for five dollars") and it writes the
ledger. We built it for Nigerian Pidgin/Yoruba first; your recordings make
Shona the next language it truly understands.

## Step 1 — correct the sentences (send text back first)

For each numbered line: read the **meaning**, then rewrite the draft as
**exactly what a trader at a market stall in Zimbabwe would really say**.
Mix in English words wherever that is natural — most people mix, and the
app is built for that. Please don't "clean it up" into textbook Shona;
natural beats correct.

One question first, because it changes the money words:

> **Q: What money do traders quote prices in day to day — US dollars, ZiG,
> rand? And how do people say amounts out loud?** (e.g. "five dollars",
> "two fifty", "mazana maviri"…) Tell us what's real; the app will use that.

### Logging sales

1. **Meaning:** I sold three cups of rice for five (dollars) fifty.
   Draft: *Ndatengesa three cups dze rice five fifty.*
2. **Meaning:** Sold one bag of maize for forty-five (dollars).
   Draft: *Ndatengesa one bag ye chibage forty five dollars.*
3. **Meaning:** A customer took two buckets of groundnuts at two fifty EACH.
   (Say it the compressed way people really say prices, if there is one.)
   Draft: *Customer atora two buckets dze nzungu two fifty each.*
4. **Meaning:** Sold tomatoes for three (dollars) — no quantity mentioned.
   Draft: *Ndatengesa madomasi three dollars.*
5. **Meaning:** Two crates of eggs, ten (dollars) each.
   Draft: *Two crates dze mazai ten dollars each.*
6. **Meaning:** Sold sugar, one kilo, one fifty.
   Draft: *Ndatengesa shuga one kilo one fifty.*

### Spending money

7. **Meaning:** I bought transport/fuel for ten (dollars).
   Draft: *Ndatenga fuel ten dollars.*
8. **Meaning:** I bought a bag of packaging bags for two (dollars).
   Draft: *Ndatenga bag re maplastic two dollars.*

### Asking the book questions

9. **Meaning:** How much have I made today?
   Draft: *Ndawana marii nhasi?*
10. **Meaning:** What did I sell most this week?
    Draft: *Chii chandatengesa kupfuura zvese week ino?*
11. **Meaning:** Close the day, give me the summary.
    Draft: *Vhara zuva, ndipe summary.*

### Fixing mistakes & credit

12. **Meaning:** No no — it was five dollars, not five fifty. (correcting
    the last entry)
    Draft: *Aiwa aiwa — five dollars kwete five fifty.*
13. **Meaning:** That one was on credit — she will pay on Friday.
    Draft: *Iyoyo yaive chikwereti — anobhadhara Friday.*

### Unclear ones (record these too — they teach the app to ASK, not guess)

14. **Meaning:** "Write it down" — but with no details at all.
    Draft: *Nyora izvozvo.*
15. **Meaning:** "I've sold out the maize" — a sale with NO amount said.
    Draft: *Chibage chapera kutengeswa.*

### Your own words (the most valuable part)

16–20. **Five sales or purchases in YOUR OWN words** — real things from a
market day, said however you would actually say them, numbers included.
Don't write these down first; just talk.

21. **One sentence of ordinary chat** — anything (greeting a neighbour,
weather, gossip) that is NOT about money. (This teaches the app what to
leave out of the ledger.)

**Send the corrected text for 1–15 back first** (WhatsApp text is fine, one
numbered list). We check the numbers-words against the app's grammar, agree
the final wording, and only then:

## Step 2 — record the clips

- Phone voice recorder is perfect. A bit of market/background noise is
  GOOD — that's real life. Just don't shout over loud music.
- **One sentence per clip**, said at your natural speaking pace — as if the
  stall is busy and you're telling your phone quickly.
- Say the FINAL corrected wording (not the drafts).
- Name them by number if easy (01, 02 …) or just send in order —
  WhatsApp voice notes or files, either works.
- 21 clips total. If one comes out wrong, just record it again; send the
  one you like.

That's everything. These clips do two jobs: they become the test that the
app must pass before Shona goes live, and (only if you agree separately)
some may be offered to the speech-model benchmark. Nothing is shared
anywhere without your explicit yes.
