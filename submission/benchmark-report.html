SautiLedger: Code-Switch Speech Benchmark

Sahara CodeSwitch Africa Challenge, Phase 2 · David Agbolade · 7 speech systems on code-switched African market and broadcast speech, scored on whether the money survives, not only the words · Full report, data card and code: github.com/dagbolade/sautiledger (bench/results/REPORT.md, bench/DATASET.md)
Summary.
  • No model wins everywhere, and in our sample the leader flips with the language (a direction, not yet a proven ranking: see section 8). On Nigerian Pidgin/Yoruba market speech, MAI-Transcribe-2 records the most transactions exactly (60%), ahead of GPT-4o-transcribe (53%) and Sahara v2.5 (47%), all three with 0% corrupted amounts. On Shona the order inverts: Sahara v2.5 and Meta's open omnilingual-ASR tie at 27% exact with 0% corrupted, double the best frontier system, while three frontier systems produce wrong parser amounts.
  • WER hid the Shona failure; the task metric exposed it. Sahara's Shona WER (0.566) is slightly better than its Pidgin WER (0.574), yet its transaction accuracy falls from 47% to 27%. It keeps the Shona words and loses the English price ("ne five dollars fifty" → ne50).
  • Sahara v2.5 has the lowest WER on all three frozen tiers, but not everywhere: Chirp-3 is better on AfriSwitch Yoruba and Hausa, and on a supplementary Yoruba-English farm tier from a second speaker Sahara is mid-field (section 7).
  • Safety has to be engineered, not inferred from accuracy. A plausible-but-wrong transcript is more dangerous than a garbled one, because only the plausible one parses. Our grammar turns uncertainty into a question; on Shona, Sahara produced 15/15 amount-safe outcomes.

Total: 70 clips, 10.5 audio-minutes. The 30 native clips cover 15 transactions, 5 cases where the correct response is to ask (e.g. a sale with no price), 4 ledger queries, 4 corrections and 2 daily summaries. Preprocessing: phone audio converted to 16 kHz mono 16-bit PCM WAV, with quality gates (1-15 s, not silent); AfriSwitch audio used as published. Text normalised for scoring: lower-case, punctuation removed, whitespace collapsed, diacritics folded (so a model is not penalised for correct Yoruba tone marks the reference lacks). Raw, unnormalised scores are in the full report. Frozen: the manifest sha256 50e5e064…d4377aeb was fixed before the first model ran, and no clip was added or dropped afterwards.

Field use (evidence, not a scored tier). The live app was used on phones from 26 Aug to 12 Sep: 39 device sessions, 217 utterances (91 spoken), 50 ledger entries and 91 clarifying questions. These counts include our own test sessions, which cannot be cleanly separated from testers'. Nine conversation scenarios (section 6) replay real sessions word for word: 6 now complete after the fixes they prompted, and 3 still fail, none with a wrong amount.

2. Systems and metrics

Systems (September 2026): Intron Sahara v2.5 (sync API, explicit use_language_asr_input); MAI-Transcribe-2 (Microsoft), GPT-4o-transcribe (OpenAI), Chirp-3 (Google) and Parakeet-TDT 0.6B v3 (NVIDIA), all via OpenRouter; Whisper-large-v3 (local) and Meta omnilingual-ASR CTC-300M (local, open weights). Every transcript is cached, so all scores can be recomputed without new API calls.

6. Beyond transcription: the full loop

Conversation benchmark (agent + real ledger)

37 scripted sessions replayed through the real agent and SQLite ledger: clarifying questions, confirmations, rejections and repairs included. 16 of 19 tasks completed, none ending with a wrong amount. 18 of 18 controls correctly did not complete. 17 of those are real, unscripted Shona market chatter (greetings, "your tomatoes aren't fresh", "won't you give me a little extra?"), replayed exactly as Sahara transcribed it: 0 ledger rows written. Five scenarios start with a deliberately wrong amount and repair it. Wrong amounts are counted at every turn, not only at the end, so a repaired mistake still shows up.

TTS round-trip (Sahara TTS)

The app reads every entry back aloud. We synthesised its real confirmations and transcribed them with an independent ASR (Whisper-small). The first run caught a product bug: the app was speaking its punctuation ("expense: fuel" came back as "expense call on"), which corrupted the amount. After the fix: hallucination 0.117 → 0.000, amount survival 91% → 100% (Pidgin voice; Yoruba-accent voice 91% → 100%).

7. Qualitative findings

  • The meaning-reversal error. Four of the seven systems transcribe Pidgin's perfective "I don sell" ("I have sold") as "I don't sell". WER counts that as one wrong word, but it reverses the transaction. Of the three such sentences in the corpus, Sahara v2.5 kept the meaning in all three; GPT-4o in two (the third became "I no sell", also a negation).
  • Which half of a code-switched sentence survives depends on the model. On Shona, Sahara keeps the Shona and loses the English price; Chirp-3 does the opposite. A ledger can ask for a missing amount but cannot recover a missing item, an asymmetry WER cannot see.
  • The ranking does not transfer. Benchmark only on Pidgin and you pick MAI-Transcribe-2, which corrupts 13% of Shona amounts; only on Shona and you pick Sahara and lose 13 points on Pidgin. Route by language, and measure per language and task.
  • A second speaker changes the picture again. A supplementary tier (recorded 14 Sep, own hash, outside the frozen corpus): 15 Yoruba-English farm-trade lines from a second Nigerian speaker, labelled before recording. WER: Parakeet .660, GPT-4o .696, MAI .742, Sahara .747, Chirp-3 .763, omnilingual .807, Whisper .847, no gap significant; omnilingual's CER (.339) is significantly below Sahara's (.525, p=.035). Exact transactions are near zero for all: models fuse mo ta into Mota and anglicise items (isu → issue), yet amounts stay safe 80-93% of the time. The main surviving corruption is a pack gap: ikan je ("each") is unknown. References are her adapted script; two lines with a different spoken price are reported separately.
  • A registered prediction, partly falsified. Before any Shona audio existed, we predicted (in a committed file) that Sahara would degrade on Shona, which is a supported language but not a documented code-switch pair. On WER we were wrong; on transactions we were right.

8. Limitations and product feedback

  • Small and confounded. 15 clips per native tier, one speaker each, so language is confounded with voice, microphone and room. Our explanation for the Pidgin/Shona reversal (Pidgin is lexically close to English, Shona is not) is a hypothesis; testing it would need the same speakers across languages. The speech is read, not live trade.
  • Statistical confidence. Paired exact McNemar/sign tests with bootstrap 95% intervals (python -m bench.significance). No transaction-accuracy gap is significant at n=15 (MAI vs Sahara: 4 Pidgin clips differ, 3-1, p=.63). What holds: Sahara's Shona WER is below GPT-4o (p=.04), Parakeet (p=.01), Chirp-3 (p=.02) and Whisper (p<.001), and its broadcast WER below MAI's (p=.04).
  • Script, not verbatim. In 4 of 15 Pidgin clips the speaker added openers ("How far na") the reference lacks, inflating tier-a WER for all models. Without them Sahara's WER falls from .574 to .358 and it stays first; only two adjacent pairs swap. Labels are unaffected; the frozen references are unchanged.
  • Moving targets and a null result. Sahara's output on identical audio changed between 5 Aug and 9 Sep (broadcast WER .424 → .369) with no version field; AfriSwitch was republished on 7 Sep, after our sample. use_disable_llm_corrections=TRUE changed nothing on all 70 clips. A re-run on 14 Sep, after Intron made the language parameter mandatory, returned byte-identical transcripts from Sahara, Chirp-3 and Parakeet; GPT-4o changed 60 of 70 on the same audio, moving its Pidgin exact rate from 53% to 47%.
  • Feedback to Intron: add a model-version field; reject unknown use_* fields (a misspelt language parameter silently fell back to English); make Shona a code-switch pair (AfriSwitch already has 3.86 h at CMI 24.55); fix streaming COMMIT, which returns INPUT_ERROR.

9. What we did with the result

SautiLedger runs on Sahara v2.5 for speech input and read-back, and the benchmark shows why that is a reasoned choice rather than a default. Sahara has the lowest WER on the three frozen tiers, ties for first on Shona with no corrupted amounts, and was the only system to transcribe all three Pidgin perfective sentences with the verb intact and not negated. It is also the only vendor here that provides TTS in the same Nigerian register, which the spoken read-back depends on. It is not the best transcriber of Pidgin transactions: MAI-Transcribe-2 is. Our engineering answer is to make the ledger safe whatever the ASR does: one gate sits in front of every write, no amount is recorded unless it appears in the transcript, and the app asks when unsure. It cannot undo an ASR error that turns one plausible amount into another, which is why every entry is read back. The next step the data points to is per-language routing, and a pack entry for Yoruba ikan je.

Reproduce: python -m bench.run --score-only rebuilds every score from cached transcripts; python -m bench.conversations replays the sessions. python -m bench.supplementary --score-only scores the farm tier. All 45 native clips (three speakers) are published with each speaker's consent, with transcripts and ledger labels, at huggingface.co/datasets/dagbolade/sautiledger-market-speech; AfriSwitch audio is not redistributed.
